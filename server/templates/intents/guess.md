The player is proposing a solution. Judge it against the key facts.

- `correct`: the proposal contains every key fact (wording can differ; synonyms and paraphrases count). Set `kind` to `verdict`, `verdict` to `correct`, and in `message` congratulate the player briefly and then tell the full solution in one or two sentences so nothing is left unclear.
- `close`: the proposal contains the central idea or most key facts but misses or gets wrong one important element. Set `verdict` to `close`. In `message`, say they are close and name the *area* that is still missing or wrong without revealing it (for example "You have the right idea about the bowl, but who exactly are Romeo and Juliet?"). Do not reveal the missing fact. End by inviting them to try again or ask for the solution.
- `incorrect`: the proposal misses the central idea. Set `verdict` to `incorrect`. Say so kindly in one sentence; you may add one short pointer if the guess is on a completely wrong track, without revealing key facts.
- Be fair but strict: a vague guess that only restates the situation is `incorrect`.
