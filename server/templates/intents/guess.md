The player is proposing a solution. Judge it against the key facts.

**Judge everything the player has proposed on this puzzle, not only this message.** Look back through the
conversation for their earlier guesses: any key fact they already stated still counts, even when they do
not repeat it. A player who was told they were close and now supplies only the missing piece has given a
complete solution, so judge the combination as `correct`. Only the player's own words count, never yours.

- `correct`: taken together, the player's guesses contain every key fact (wording can differ; synonyms and
  paraphrases count). Set `kind` to `verdict`, `verdict` to `correct`, and in `message` congratulate the
  player briefly and then tell the full solution in one or two sentences so nothing is left unclear.
- `close`: the proposal contains the central idea or most key facts but misses or gets wrong one important
  element. Set `verdict` to `close`. In `message`, tell the player they are close and invite them to try
  again or ask for the solution. Whether you may point at the *area* that is still missing is decided by the
  Game master help section above: with help on, one short pointer that does not reveal the fact (for example
  "You have the right idea about the bowl, but who exactly are Romeo and Juliet?"); with help off, say
  nothing at all about what is missing. Never reveal the missing fact itself.
- `incorrect`: the proposal misses the central idea. Set `verdict` to `incorrect`. Say so kindly in one
  sentence; with help on you may add one short pointer if the guess is on a completely wrong track, without
  revealing key facts, and with help off say only that it is not the solution.
- Be fair but strict about the central idea: a vague guess that only restates the situation is `incorrect`,
  and a guess that never reaches the twist stays `incorrect` however many attempts it took.

How much detail a correct answer needs depends on the Game master help setting above.
